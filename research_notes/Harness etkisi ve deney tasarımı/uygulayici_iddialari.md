# Practitioner claims: why the harness matters for coding agents, and what evidence backs them

Scope note (researcher): this covers the period up to 2026-11-23. Verification labels used below:
- **[VERBATIM-PRIMARY]**: exact text pulled from the primary source itself (for X posts, the full text came from the fxtwitter API mirror of the original post).
- **[VERBATIM-AUTOTRANSCRIPT]**: exact text from an automatic (Whisper-style) YouTube transcript. The wording is close to what was said, but it contains transcription errors (shown with [sic]).
- **[EXTRACTED]**: sentences quoted by a fetch tool that summarized the primary page. They are probably verbatim, but spot-check before printing them as quotes.
- **[PARAPHRASE]** / **[SECONDARY]**: not verified against a primary source.

Dimension tags used for each claim: **ACC** = benchmark/task accuracy, **LONG** = long sessions / multi-context-window work, **COST** = tokens / $ / latency, **REL** = reliability / compounding errors / safety, **HITL** = human-in-the-loop usability / verification speed.

## 1. Andrej Karpathy: context engineering, LLM OS, partial autonomy and the autonomy slider, the generation-verification loop, "on a leash", and agents' limits

### Takeaway
Karpathy's "harness matters" case is mainly about **HITL usability and reliability**, not benchmark accuracy. Across 2025 he argued that an LLM app is "a thick layer of non-trivial software" around the model: context engineering, orchestration, GUIs, and an autonomy slider. He said that layer exists because models are fallible ("jagged", "cognitive deficits", no continual learning), so humans must verify quickly and keep the AI "on the leash". In January 2026 he reported a personal phase shift to "80% agent coding", which he credited to **model** capability ("threshold of coherence"). His remaining complaints in that post are about behaviour that harness instructions (CLAUDE.md) did not fix. He has given no measurements. Everything is first-person experience and argument.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Context engineering over prompt engineering: X post, 2025-06-25 [VERBATIM-PRIMARY]** — [x.com/karpathy/status/1937902205765607626](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1937902205765607626) (full text retrieved through api.fxtwitter.com; timestamp Wed Jun 25 15:54:24 UTC 2025)
- "+1 for "context engineering" over "prompt engineering"."
- "in every industrial-strength LLM app, context engineering is the delicate art and science of filling the context window with just the right information for the next step. Science because doing this right involves task descriptions and explanations, few shot examples, RAG, related (possibly multimodal) data, tools, state and history, compacting... Too little or of the wrong form and the LLM doesn't have the right context for optimal performance. Too much or too irrelevant and the LLM costs might go up and performance might come down." (ACC and COST, both stated as principle, not measured)
- "On top of context engineering itself, an LLM app has to: break up problems just right into control flows; pack the context windows just right; dispatch calls to LLMs of the right kind and capability; handle generation-verification UIUX flows; a lot more - guardrails, security, evals, parallelism, prefetching, ..."
- "So context engineering is just one small piece of an emerging thick layer of non-trivial software that coordinates individual LLM calls (and a lot more) into full LLM apps. The term "ChatGPT wrapper" is tired and really, really wrong."
- Context: he was amplifying Tobi Lütke's post of ~2025-06-19 (date decoded from the post ID 1935533422589399127), which preferred the term "context engineering" as "the art of providing all the context for the task to be plausibly solvable by the LLM". Simon Willison's 2025-06-27 write-up covers both — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/jun/27/context-engineering/); [Tobi post](https://twitter.com/tobi/status/1935533422589399127)

### Inferences
- Karpathy's position changed between Oct 2025 ("slop", "just not net useful") and Jan 2026 ("80% agent coding"). He attributes the change to **model** improvement, not harness change. Yet his advice for getting value is all harness or workflow: success criteria, tests-first, a browser in the loop, plan mode, an IDE beside the agent. Read together, his view is "the model unlocks it, the harness and loop design extract it".
- The "thick layer" argument (June 2025) is a claim about **product value and HITL usability**: GUIs, verification speed, autonomy control. It is not a claim that harnesses raise benchmark scores.
- His statement that CLAUDE.md instructions did not fix behavioural problems supports this project's own decision note that "warning-text layers do not make a difference on strong models".

## 2. What vendors and builders claim the harness contributes (Anthropic, OpenAI, Cognition, Manus, Willison, Ball/Amp, Cursor, Zechner/pi, LangChain, Bölük)

### Takeaway
Builders mostly agree that harness value sits in four places:
1. **Context management.** Curating and compacting context, retrieving just in time, sharing context rather than splitting it.
2. **Tool and interface design.** The ACI and edit formats.
3. **Verification and feedback loops.** Tests, browser checks, completion checklists, linters.
4. **State across sessions.** Progress files, git, feature lists.

The motivation is mostly **REL and LONG**, with **COST** (KV-cache, tokens) a strong secondary theme. Only a few posts give controlled numbers: Anthropic multi-agent research, Cursor semantic search, LangChain Terminal-Bench, and Bölük's edit formats. The rest is design reasoning and anecdote from production.

### Cited Findings

**Anthropic**
- *Building effective agents*, 2024-12-19, Erik Schluntz and Barry Zhang [EXTRACTED]. "we recommend finding the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed". There are three principles: simplicity, transparency ("explicitly showing the agent's planning steps") and a carefully crafted **agent-computer interface (ACI)**. For their SWE-bench agent, "we actually spent more time optimizing our tools than the overall prompt"; they "changed the tool to always require absolute filepaths" after relative-path errors. Agents need "ground truth" from the environment at each step. "The autonomous nature of agents means higher costs, and the potential for compounding errors." Frameworks "create extra layers of abstraction that can obscure the underlying prompts and responses." (ACC anecdote; REL, COST.) No ablation numbers are given. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- *How we built our multi-agent research system*, 2025-06-13, Hadfield, Zhang, Lien, Scholz, Fox, Ford [EXTRACTED; **measured on internal evals**]. The multi-agent system (Opus 4 lead plus Sonnet 4 subagents) "outperformed single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2%" on an internal research eval. On BrowseComp, "token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance" (95% together with tool-call count and model choice). Multi-agent uses "about 15× more tokens than chats", and agents about 4×. A tool-testing agent rewrote tool descriptions, giving "a 40% decrease in task completion time". Parallelism cut research time by up to 90%. **Caveat for coding:** coding tasks have fewer parallelizable subtasks, so multi-agent "is not a good fit" for many coding domains. (ACC, COST, REL) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- *Effective context engineering for AI agents*, 2025-09-29, Rajasekaran, Dixon, Ryan, Hadfield [EXTRACTED]. Context is "a finite resource with diminishing marginal returns". It describes "context rot", an "attention budget", and the goal of "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of some desired outcome". Techniques: compaction, structured note-taking (Claude plays Pokémon), sub-agents that return summaries of 1,000–2,000 tokens, and just-in-time retrieval. Claude Code is hybrid: CLAUDE.md is loaded up front, and glob/grep fetch files at runtime. **On model progress:** "smarter models require less prescriptive engineering". The direction is "letting intelligent models act intelligently, with progressively less human curation"; "do the simplest thing that works". No benchmark numbers. (LONG, COST) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- *Effective harnesses for long-running agents*, 2025-11-26, Justin Young [EXTRACTED]. Two parts: an **initializer agent** that sets up the environment, writes a JSON feature list (200+ features, all marked "failing") and a progress file, and a **coding agent** that makes "incremental progress in every session, while leaving clear artifacts for the next session", using git to revert. JSON was chosen because "the model is less likely to inappropriately change or overwrite JSON files compared to Markdown files". Failure modes seen: trying to one-shot ("tended to try to do too much at once"), premature victory ("declare the job done"), and marking features complete "without proper testing". Browser-automation testing "dramatically improved performance". **No quantitative results.** The post admits that multi-context-window progress "remains an open problem". (LONG, REL; anecdotal) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- *Quantifying infrastructure noise in agentic coding evals*, 2026-02-05, Gian Segato [EXTRACTED; **measured**]. On Terminal-Bench 2.0, resource configuration **alone** moved scores by 6 points (p < 0.01). Infra error rates went from 5.8% (strict) to 0.5% (uncapped). On SWE-bench, 5× RAM gave only +1.54 points (227 problems × 10 samples). Advice: treat leaderboard gaps under 3 points skeptically. "A few-point lead might signal a real capability gap—or it might just be a bigger VM." (ACC, as a measurement confound) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise)

**OpenAI**
- *Harness engineering: leveraging Codex in an agent-first world*, 2026-02-11, Ryan Lopopolo [EXTRACTED]. An internal beta product was built with **zero manually written code**: about 1,500 merged PRs and "on the order of a million lines of code" over about 5 months (first commit late August 2025), by 3 engineers growing to 7, about 3.5 PRs per engineer per day. They estimate it took about 1/10 the time of hand-writing (**an estimate, not measured**). "Humans steer. Agents execute." AGENTS.md (about 100 lines) is treated as "the table of contents" pointing into `docs/`. "anything it can't access in-context while running effectively doesn't exist". Architecture invariants are enforced by custom linters whose error messages contain the fix instructions. Background "garbage-collection" agents replaced a 20%-of-the-week human cleanup that "didn't scale". The hardest remaining problem is "designing environments, feedback loops, and control systems". Single Codex runs of 6+ hours are said to be regular (anecdote). (LONG, REL, HITL) — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/)

**Cognition (Devin)**
- *Don't Build Multi-Agents*, 2025-06-12, Walden Yan [VERBATIM-PRIMARY via Wayback copy]. "When agents have to actually be reliable while running for long periods of time and maintain coherent conversations, there are certain things you must do to contain the potential for compounding errors. ... At the core of reliability is Context Engineering." "'Context engineering' ... is effectively the #1 job of engineers building AI agents." Principle 1: "Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages". Principle 2: "Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results". "The simplest way to follow the principles is to just use a single-threaded linear agent". For long tasks, use a compression model ("you might even consider fine-tuning a smaller model (this is in fact something we've done at Cognition)"). On Claude Code subagents (as of June 2025), the subagent "is usually only tasked with answering a question, not writing any code"; "The designers of Claude Code took a purposefully simple approach." On edit-apply models: these were common in 2024, but "Today, the edit decision-making and applying are more often done by a single model in one action" (an example of a harness component absorbed by the model). **Evidence: a thought experiment (Flappy Bird) and production experience. No numbers.** (REL, LONG) — [Cognition (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://cognition.ai/blog/dont-build-multi-agents). Note: the live cognition.ai page did not resolve from this machine (DNS failure), so I read the Wayback copy.
- Tension: Anthropic's measured +90.2% multi-agent result (research tasks) appeared one day later and points the other way. Both agree that coding is a poor fit for parallel multi-agent setups.

**Manus**
- *Context Engineering for AI Agents: Lessons from Building Manus*, 2025-07-18, Yichao "Peak" Ji [EXTRACTED]. "the KV-cache hit rate is the single most important metric for a production-stage AI agent". With Claude Sonnet, cached input costs 0.30 USD/MTok against 3 USD/MTok uncached (list prices). Input:output is about 100:1, with about 50 tool calls per task. Advice: mask tools rather than removing them, use the file system as context ("unlimited in size, persistent by nature"), recite goals via todo.md, keep failed steps in context ("error recovery is one of the clearest indicators of true agentic behavior"), and don't "few-shot yourself into a rut". "we've rebuilt our agent framework four times". The strategic reason to bet on context engineering over fine-tuning: "If model progress is the rising tide, we want Manus to be the boat, not the pillar stuck to the seabed." **No ablations. Cost figures come from public pricing.** (COST, LONG, REL) — [Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)

**Simon Willison**
- *Designing agentic loops*, 2025-09-30 [EXTRACTED/PARAPHRASE]. He calls designing agentic loops a critical new skill. An agent "runs tools in a loop to achieve a goal". Loops work best with "clear success criteria" and automated tests. YOLO mode makes agents much more productive but carries three risks: destructive commands, exfiltration, and use of your machine for attacks. So sandbox the agent (Docker, Codespaces), use scoped credentials and budget limits, and prefer CLI tools documented in AGENTS.md over MCP. (REL/safety, HITL; experiential) — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Sep/30/designing-agentic-loops/)
- His 2025-06-27 post endorsed the term "context engineering" — [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/jun/27/context-engineering/)

**Thorsten Ball / Amp**
- *How to Build an Agent, or: The Emperor Has No Clothes*, 2025-04-15 [EXTRACTED]. "It's not that hard to build a fully functioning, code-editing agent." "It's an LLM, a loop, and enough tokens." "less than 400 lines of code, most of which is boilerplate" (300 lines, three tools). "The rest, the stuff that makes Amp so addictive and impressive? Elbow grease." This means the basic loop is commodity, and the product difference lies in "practical engineering". (HITL / product polish; no measurement) — [Amp](https://ampcode.com/how-to-build-an-agent)

**Cursor**
- *Improving agent with semantic search*, 2025-11-06 [SECONDARY via search summary of the primary page; **measured**]. Semantic search raised answer accuracy by about 12.5% on average (6.5%–23.5% depending on the model) on their offline Cursor Context Bench. Online A/B tests showed much smaller effects: +0.3% code retention overall, +2.6% on codebases with 1,000+ files, and +2.2% dissatisfied follow-ups without it. Grep and semantic search together worked best. (ACC; the offline-vs-online gap is noteworthy) — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/semsearch)

**Mario Zechner (pi)**
- *What I learned building an opinionated and minimal coding agent*, 2025-11-30 (benchmark section updated 2025-12-02) [EXTRACTED]. "pi's system prompt and tool definitions together come in below 1000 tokens"; "there does not appear to be a need for 10,000 tokens of system prompt". Four tools (read, write, edit, bash) "are all you need for an effective coding agent". No MCP, no subagent tool, no plan mode, no built-in to-dos. "Exactly controlling what goes into the model's context yields better outputs, especially when it's writing code." "I want to inspect every aspect of my interactions with the model." The reason minimal works: "all the frontier models have been RL-trained up the wazoo, so they inherently understand what a coding agent is", and "In all likelihood, the models have some training on their native coding harness." Claude Code "has turned into a spaceship with 80% of functionality I have no use for." He ran a full Terminal-Bench 2.0 submission (Opus 4.5, 5 trials per task). The text says pi and Terminus 2 hold "their own against agents with far more sophisticated tooling", but **the actual scores are only in images and results.json, which I did not read**. (Transparency/HITL, COST; claims of ACC parity) — [mariozechner.at](https://mariozechner.at/posts/2025-11-30-pi-coding-agent/)

**LangChain (deepagents-cli)**
- *Improving Deep Agents with harness engineering*, 2026-02-17, Vivek Trivedy [EXTRACTED; **measured**]. Terminal-Bench 2.0 (89 tasks), model fixed at gpt-5.2-codex: **52.8% → 66.5% (+13.7 points)**, "We only changed the harness." Changes: a self-verification prompt plus `PreCompletionChecklistMiddleware`, `LocalContextMiddleware` (maps the directory at startup), `LoopDetectionMiddleware` (per-file edit counts), time-budget warnings, and a "reasoning sandwich" (xhigh/high/xhigh; xhigh alone scored 53.9% because of timeouts vs 63.6% at high). **No per-component ablation and no trial count stated.** They warn that task-specific changes "are bad for generalization". Loop detection is "a design heuristic that engineers around today's perceived model issues". Opus 4.6 on an earlier harness version scored 59.6%. (ACC) — [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/blog/improving-deep-agents-with-harness-engineering)

**Can Bölük (Stencil), "The Harness Problem"**
- *We improved 15 LLMs at coding in one afternoon. Only the harness changed.*, 2026-02-12 [EXTRACTED; **measured, self-built benchmark**]. 16 models, 180 tasks × 3 runs, comparing edit formats patch / str_replace / "hashline" (lines addressed by a short content hash). Grok Code Fast 1 went from 6.7% to 68.3%. MiniMax M2.1 gained +41.7 points over patch. Gemini 3 Flash gained about 8 points. Hashline averaged about +15 points over patch, and output tokens fell by up to 61% (Grok 4 Fast). Weak models gained the most. Total cost was about $300. He argues vendors restricting third-party harnesses (for example the Anthropic/OpenCode dispute) slows harness innovation. **Limits:** synthetic mutation bugs in React files, a success criterion of exact restoration, an author-built benchmark, no CIs. (ACC, COST) — [Stencil / blog.can.ac](https://stencil.so/blog/the-harness-problem)

### Inferences
- The claims sort into three groups:
  1. **Measured ACC gains with the model held fixed**: LangChain +13.7 pts, Bölük up to +61 pts on weak models, Cursor +12.5% offline, Anthropic multi-agent +90.2% on research.
  2. **LONG/REL architecture claims**, all anecdotal: Anthropic's long-running harness, Cognition, Manus, OpenAI.
  3. **COST claims**: Manus KV-cache, Anthropic 15× tokens, Bölük −61% tokens.
- Even the measured gains mostly fix **expression/interface failures**: edit-format errors, early stopping without verification, doom loops, timeouts. They do not supply new capability. This fits Bölük's line: "the model isn't flaky at understanding the task. It's flaky at expressing itself."
- Several builders (Ball, Zechner, mini-swe-agent, Willison) agree the **core loop is trivial**. They locate value either in polish and HITL (Ball, Karpathy) or in transparency and control over context (Zechner). This matches this project's "transparent harness" vision.

## 3. Counter-arguments: "the model is the product", the bitter lesson, minimal harnesses, and shrinking harness advantages

### Takeaway
There is a strong, credible counter-position, much of it voiced by the harness builders themselves. Frontier models are RL-trained on agentic coding, so minimal harnesses (mini-swe-agent's ~100 lines plus bash, pi's <1k-token prompt) get close to frontier scores. Anthropic itself writes that "smarter models require less prescriptive engineering". Components such as edit-apply models have already been absorbed into models. The Claude Code team's "bitter lesson" framing (scaffolding gains of ~10–20% get wiped out by the next model) is widely reported but **unverified at the primary level**. The best 2026 measurements are mixed. Harness swaps can move frontier-model scores by 7–15 points. A controlled study with matched open models found harness changes mainly moved **cost** (up to 40× tokens per solved task), with pass-rate differences of 0–8 points, mostly within noise.

### Cited Findings
- **mini-swe-agent** (SWE-agent team, Princeton/Stanford) README [EXTRACTED]: "Just some 100 lines of python for the agent class". "Scores >74% on the SWE-bench verified benchmark". "Does not have any tools other than bash" and "doesn't even need to use the tool-calling interface". "Has a completely linear history." The rationale: "as LMs have become more capable, a lot of this is not needed at all to build a useful agent!" and "What if our agent was 100x simpler, and still worked nearly as well?" The design "puts the language model (rather than the agent scaffold) in the middle of our attention". — [GitHub](https://github.com/SWE-agent/mini-swe-agent). Note: the Zhang et al. position paper (below) cites third-party monitors recommending mini-SWE-agent as the locked harness for cross-model comparison — [arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950)
- **Zechner:** frontier models are "RL-trained up the wazoo", so a minimal harness is enough. Terminus 2 (the Terminal-Bench team's minimal agent) "holding its own against agents with far more sophisticated tooling". — [mariozechner.at](https://mariozechner.at/posts/2025-11-30-pi-coding-agent/)
- **Anthropic on shrinking need:** "smarter models require less prescriptive engineering"; the future is "progressively less human curation". — [Anthropic, 2025-09-29](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Component absorption, example:** Cognition says 2024-era "edit apply models" were needed because "many models were really bad at editing code". By June 2025 "the edit decision-making and applying are more often done by a single model in one action." — [Cognition (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://cognition.ai/blog/dont-build-multi-agents)
- **Karpathy attributes the Dec 2025 phase shift to the model** ("threshold of coherence"). He also reports that CLAUDE.md instructions failed to fix behaviour. — [X, 2026-01-26](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2015883857489522876)
- **Boris Cherny (Claude Code creator) and the bitter lesson [SECONDARY / PARAPHRASE ONLY]:** secondary notes on his YC Light Cone and Lenny's Podcast (2026-02-19) appearances say a framed copy of Sutton's "The Bitter Lesson" hangs in the Claude Code area, and that he frames the trade-off as building a ~10–20% scaffolding gain now or waiting for the next model, which often wipes it out. They also report his advice to "build for the model six months out". **I could not find a verbatim primary transcript.** The Lenny's and Pragmatic Engineer (2026-03-04) pages only list "The Bitter Lesson" as a reference. — [secondary: ayushjj knowledge graph](https://ayushjj.github.io/knowledge-graph/insight/scaffolding-is-tech-debt-against-the-next-model); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/head-of-claude-code-what-happens); [36kr summary (reports advice to clear CLAUDE.md/skills/hooks every ~6 months)](https://www.36kr.com/p/3918996932046216)
- **Manus's "boat vs pillar":** this is the opposite reading of the bitter lesson. Invest in model-agnostic context engineering *because* models keep improving, rather than fine-tuning. — [Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)
- **Evidence that the harness still matters at the frontier (2026)** — *Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness*, Zhang, Wang, Ge, Xu, Hamm, Reddy, arXiv 2605.23950, 2026-05-07 (a position paper) [VERBATIM-PRIMARY from the HTML]. "Under the standardized SEAL scaffold on SWE-bench Pro, Claude Opus 4.5 reaches 45.9%, while under Claude Code the same model reaches 55.4%." "the six leading frontier models span only 4.9 percentage points under the standardized SEAL scaffold (41.0% to 45.9%)", so the within-model harness range is about 2× the within-harness model range. Terminal-Bench 2 with GPT-5.4 (high) rose from 69.7% to 77.0% through automated harness evolution. Grok 4 on SWE-bench Verified went from 58.6% (SWE-agent) to 72–75% (xAI scaffold). Third-party monitors report 11 points (GPT-5) and 15 points (Kimi K2 Thinking) of scaffold-only variation. Their reply to "Model capability will dissolve the harness problem": "The empirical trend goes the other way: the harness ecosystem has grown more complex as models have improved". Their own factorial experiment found that some harnesses absorb model differences while others (per-step verification) "expose previously hidden model differences". They admit public evidence is observational and does not "isolate which harness components drive the variance". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950)
- **Evidence that harness effects on pass rate are small among comparable models** — *The Scaffold Effect in Coding Agents*, Vats and Golev, arXiv 2607.22585, 2026-06-08 (ICML 2026 workshop) [EXTRACTED]. Qwen 3.6 Plus and MiniMax M2.5 × Goose / OpenCode / OpenHands-SDK on a 50-task Terminal-Bench Pro subset. **Up to 40× difference in tokens per solved task**. Within-model pass-rate gaps were 0–8 points, and 95% bootstrap CIs include zero except for the largest gap. Failure "fingerprints" are specific to each harness and recur across models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22585)
- **Edit-format replication:** nwyin's edit-bench (DEV, 2026-03-23; 3 models × 3 languages × 20 tasks, single attempt) found "For current-gen models in contemporary harnesses, edit format is not the bottleneck" and "The gap between models ... dwarfs the gap between edit formats". Hashline hurt Python for gemini-3-flash (95% → 70%). The author calls the sample small and not statistically rigorous. — [DEV](https://dev.to/nwyin/hashline-vs-replace-does-the-edit-format-matter-15n2)
- **Bölük's own data supports "advantage shrinks with stronger models":** the biggest gains went to the weakest models (Grok Code Fast 1, MiniMax), and Gemini 3 Flash gained only about 8 points. — [Stencil](https://stencil.so/blog/the-harness-problem)

### Inferences
- The two camps mostly talk about **different things**:
  - "Minimal suffices" is a claim about **single-task benchmark accuracy for frontier models** (SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench).
  - "The harness matters" claims rest on **LONG, REL, COST and HITL**: multi-session continuity, verification before stopping, tokens per solved task, auditability.
  - The 2026 evidence fits this split. Pass-rate effects are modest and model-dependent. Cost and failure-mode effects are large.
- "Advantage shrinks as models improve" is supported for **interface-repair components** (edit formats, edit-apply models, prescriptive prompts, CLAUDE.md rules). It is contested for **feedback and verification structure** (LangChain's +13.7 on a frontier codex model, Claude Code vs SEAL +9.5 on Opus 4.5).
- Benchmark harness numbers are also confounded by infrastructure. Anthropic measured 6 points from resource limits alone, so harness deltas under about 3 points on Terminal-Bench are not interpretable without matched infrastructure.

### Gaps
- I found no primary, verbatim source for Boris Cherny's bitter-lesson and "10–20%" framing, and none for a Noam Brown "scaffolds will be washed away" quote. A Latent Space write-up of the Noam Brown episode attributes the scaffolding-displacement view only to an unnamed "respondent". Treat both as unverified.
- I found no longitudinal study that tracks the size of one harness's advantage across successive model generations under fixed conditions. The "shrinking" claim is inferred from cross-sectional data (weak vs strong models).
- I did not independently check mini-swe-agent's >74% figure against the current SWE-bench bash-only leaderboard.

## 4. Which claims rest on measurements, and which on anecdote

### Takeaway
Most influential "harness matters" claims rest on **anecdote or design argument**: Karpathy, Anthropic's long-running harness post, Cognition, Manus, OpenAI's harness engineering (counts but no control), Willison, Ball and Zechner. The **measured** claims are few. They are mostly fixed-model ACC or COST comparisons on benchmarks, usually from interested parties (vendor or tool author), with small samples and rarely per-component ablations or confidence intervals. No measured claim covers human-in-the-loop usability or long multi-session reliability.

### Inferences
- The strongest measured support is for **cost and efficiency** (40× tokens per solved task, −61% output tokens) and for **fixing specific failure modes** (early stopping, edit-format errors, timeouts). The weakest is for **HITL usability**: Karpathy's central theme has no measurement anywhere in this set. The same goes for **multi-session long-run reliability**, where Anthropic and OpenAI report no controlled comparison.
- Measured claims share recurring weaknesses: an interested party (a vendor tuning its own harness, a tool author's benchmark), no per-component ablation (LangChain), small task samples (50 tasks, 20 tasks per language), synthetic tasks (Bölük), offline/online divergence (Cursor: 12.5% vs 0.3%), and infrastructure confounds (Anthropic: 6 points). An experimental design for this project should keep the model fixed, ablate one component at a time, run repeated trials with confidence intervals (Wilson/bootstrap), match infrastructure, and report tokens and $ per solved task next to pass rate.

### Gaps
- I found no randomized or controlled study of harness features on human-in-the-loop productivity (verification speed, review burden). Karpathy's "generation-verification loop" thesis is untested in these sources.
- I found no controlled evidence for the multi-context-window techniques (progress files, feature lists, compaction strategies) beyond vendor anecdote.
